Frequency Division Multiplexing and Synchronous Demodulation

In order to share a single transmission medium (air, cable, fibre) among multiple signals, each signal is shifted to a distinct carrier frequency. Provided that the receiver is aware of the carrier of interest, the corresponding signal may be recovered. On this page the underlying mechanism is simulated using baseband audio signals.

1. Modulation — Multiplication in Time

Each baseband signal $m_n(t)$ is multiplied by its dedicated carrier $\cos(2\pi f_n t)$: $$\boxed{\, s_n(t) = m_n(t) \cdot \cos(2\pi f_n t) \,}.$$ This operation modulates the amplitude of the carrier by the baseband message; in the absence of a message, no carrier is transmitted (DSB-SC, Double-Sideband Suppressed-Carrier).

2. Convolution in Frequency (Modulation Theorem)

By a fundamental property of the Fourier transform, multiplication in the time domain corresponds to convolution in the frequency domain. For $\mathcal{F}\{m(t)\}=M(f)$ and $c(t)=\cos(2\pi f_c t)$, $$\mathcal{F}\{c(t)\}=C(f)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\delta(f-f_c)+\delta(f+f_c)\bigr]$$ and consequently $$s(t)=m(t)\,c(t)\;\xleftrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\;S(f)=M(f)\;\ast\;C(f) =\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[M(f-f_c)+M(f+f_c)\bigr].$$

Equivalently, convolution with a Dirac distribution translates the spectrum to that frequency. As a result, two copies of the baseband spectrum $M(f)$ are obtained, located around $+f_c$ and $-f_c$. The bandwidth of the modulated signal becomes $2W$, where $W$ is the one-sided bandwidth of $m(t)$. The anti-aliasing low-pass filter restricts $m_n(t)$ to $W = BW/2$ so that adjacent channels do not overlap.

3. Composite Broadcast — Summation

The modulated signals are summed together with channel noise $w(t)$ and presented to the transmission medium: $$z(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} m_n(t)\cos(2\pi f_n t) + w(t).$$ In the frequency domain, each channel forms a distinct "tower" around $f_n$ (see the Towers graph above). To prevent overlap between channels, the condition $|f_n - f_k| \ge BW$ must be satisfied.

4. Synchronous Demodulation — Multiplication by the Same Frequency

The receiver multiplies the composite signal by a local oscillator (LO) at frequency $f_t$: $$r(t) = z(t)\cdot\cos(2\pi f_t t).$$ Using the trigonometric identity $\cos A\cos B = \tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]$, the contribution of the target channel becomes $$m_n(t)\cos(2\pi f_n t)\cos(2\pi f_t t) = \tfrac{m_n(t)}{2}\bigl[\cos\bigl(2\pi(f_n-f_t)t\bigr)+\cos\bigl(2\pi(f_n+f_t)t\bigr)\bigr].$$ A subsequent low-pass filter (cut-off $\approx BW/2$) suppresses the high-frequency $(f_n+f_t)$ component as well as the contributions of all other channels with $|f_n-f_t|>BW/2$. Only the baseband copy of the channel satisfying $f_t \approx f_n$ remains: $$\hat{m}_n(t) = \text{LPF}\{r(t)\} \;\approx\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,m_n(t).$$ In the frequency domain, this operation corresponds to a further shift of $S(f)$ by $\pm f_t$: when $f_t = f_n$, one of the copies is brought exactly to baseband, while the remaining contributions remain at high frequencies and are eliminated by the low-pass filter.

5. Web Audio Demodulation Chain

The real-time demodulation chain implemented on this page is composed of the following blocks:

  • AudioBufferSourceNode — plays the offline-synthesised composite signal in a loop.
  • OscillatorNode (sine at $f_t$) combined with a GainNode (audio-rate parameter modulation) forms the heterodyne multiplier.
  • BiquadFilterNode cascade of three sections — a sixth-order Butterworth low-pass filter (36 dB/octave) with cut-off $BW/2$.
  • GainNode — control of the output volume.

Application Areas

  • AM/FM broadcasting: classical analogue radio is built upon the same modulation–demodulation architecture.
  • OFDM (4G/5G LTE, Wi-Fi 802.11 a/g/n/ac/ax, DVB-T, ADSL): thousands of orthogonal subcarriers are modulated in parallel. Choosing the subcarrier frequencies as $f_k = k/T$ yields $\int_0^T \cos(2\pi f_k t)\cos(2\pi f_m t)\,dt = 0$ ($k\ne m$), so that synchronous demodulation can be carried out by a single IFFT/FFT operation. This constitutes the foundation of high-rate digital communications.
  • I/Q and QAM modulation: in order to transmit two orthogonal signals on the same carrier $f_c$, $\cos$ and $\sin$ carriers are employed in parallel, doubling the spectral efficiency.
  • Cable TV and DOCSIS: the transmission of hundreds of TV channels over a single coaxial cable constitutes a pure frequency-division multiplexing application.
  • WDM (optical communications): more than 80 wavelengths are transmitted over a single fibre, each acting as a separate carrier.
  • Radar and SDR: in software-defined radios, the modulation–demodulation chain is implemented entirely in software.

Conceptual Summary

In a single sentence: multiplication in time corresponds to convolution with a Dirac distribution in frequency, and therefore to a translation of the spectrum. Modulation shifts the spectrum upwards; synchronous demodulation, through multiplication by the same $f_c$, shifts it back to baseband while also generating a $2f_c$ component, which is removed by the low-pass filter. This principle constitutes the mathematical core of all carrier-based communications, ranging from simple radio receivers to modern OFDM-based broadband wireless systems.
