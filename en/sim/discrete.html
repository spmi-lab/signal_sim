Discrete-Time Convolution

$$y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[k]\,h[n-k]$$ The relation above defines the discrete-time convolution between the input signal $x[n]$ and the impulse response $h[n]$, and constitutes the output of the LTI system. In the third panel, the $h$ signal may be dragged with the mouse so that the product–sum operation for each value of $n$ can be examined directly. In the fourth panel, the red marker indicates the sample of $y[n]$ corresponding to the current shift position.

Notation (discrete time): the independent variable is taken as $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the normalised angular frequency as $\omega \in [-\pi, \pi]$ (rad/sample) and the normalised frequency as $f = \omega/(2\pi)$ (cycles/sample).

📍 Application Areas

  • Digital filtering: the implementation of FIR filters is realised through the convolution of each sample with $h[n]$.
  • Image processing: two-dimensional convolution (edge detection, blurring) and the convolutional layers of deep-learning networks are built upon this operation.
  • Audio processing: reverberation, echo, equalisation and impulse-response-based effects are obtained by this operation.
  • Machine learning: feature extraction in CNN architectures is performed by means of one-dimensional (audio) and two-dimensional (image) convolutions.
  • Communications: the matched filter is implemented as the convolution of a known template with the received signal.
  • Seismology: the convolution of the source signal with the medium response yields the measured seismogram.
