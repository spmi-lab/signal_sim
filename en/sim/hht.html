Hilbert-Huang Transform & Empirical Mode Decomposition

1. The Motivation for EMD

The Fourier transform represents a signal as a sum of sinusoidal basis functions of fixed frequency and presupposes that the signal is linear and stationary. The wavelet transform relaxes these assumptions but still requires a pre-selected basis function (e.g. Daubechies or Morlet). By contrast, Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) derives the basis directly from the data, without any predefined basis function. Owing to this property, EMD is specifically designed for nonlinear and non-stationary signals and decomposes a signal into its natural oscillation modes, referred to as Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs).

2. IMF (Intrinsic Mode Function) Criteria

According to Huang's 1998 definition, an IMF is required to satisfy the following two conditions:

  • Condition 1: the number of local extrema and the number of zero crossings must be equal or differ by at most one.
  • Condition 2: at every instant, the mean of the upper envelope $e_{\max}(t)$ (interpolating the local maxima) and the lower envelope $e_{\min}(t)$ (interpolating the local minima) must be approximately zero: $\tfrac{e_{\max}(t) + e_{\min}(t)}{2} \approx 0$.

Intuitively, an IMF represents an oscillatory "mode" of narrow bandwidth, characterised by well-defined instantaneous amplitude and instantaneous frequency.

3. Sifting Algorithm (Sifting Process)

EMD extracts all IMFs of the signal through the following iterative process:

  1. Start with $h_0(t) = x(t)$.
  2. Find the local maxima and minima.
  3. Construct the upper envelope $e_{\max}(t)$ via cubic spline through the maxima, and the lower envelope $e_{\min}(t)$ through the minima.
  4. Subtract the mean: $h_1(t) = h_0(t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(e_{\max}(t) + e_{\min}(t)\bigr)$.
  5. Does $h_1$ satisfy the IMF criteria? If yes, $\mathrm{IMF}_1 = h_1$. If not, set $h_0 \leftarrow h_1$ and go back to step 2.
  6. Stopping criterion (Huang): standard deviation $\mathrm{SD} = \sum_t \frac{|h_{k-1}(t) - h_k(t)|^2}{h_{k-1}(t)^2 + \epsilon}$ falls below a certain threshold (typically 0.2-0.3).
  7. Compute the residual $r_1(t) = x(t) - \mathrm{IMF}_1$, set $x \leftarrow r_1$ and go back to step 1.
  8. The process ends when the residual has fewer than 3 extrema (monotone or single wave).

In the end, the decomposition $x(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \mathrm{IMF}_i(t) + r(t)$ is obtained. By construction, IMF₁ carries the highest frequency mode, IMF_M the lowest; the residual gives the signal's trend.

4. Hilbert Spectrum (The "H" Part of HHT)

After EMD decomposes the signal into IMFs, the Hilbert transform is applied to each IMF: for $\mathrm{IMF}_i(t)$, the analytic signal $z_i(t) = \mathrm{IMF}_i(t) + j\,\mathcal{H}[\mathrm{IMF}_i](t)$ is constructed. From this:

  • Instantaneous amplitude: $a_i(t) = |z_i(t)|$
  • Instantaneous phase: $\phi_i(t) = \arg z_i(t)$
  • Instantaneous frequency: $\omega_i(t) = \frac{d\phi_i}{dt}$

When the $(t, \omega_i, a_i)$ triplets of all IMFs are combined, the Hilbert spectrum is formed — a time-frequency-energy distribution. Unlike the wavelet's fixed basis function, the frequency resolution is determined not by the Heisenberg principle but by the data's local oscillation. (This page currently shows only the EMD decomposition; the Hilbert spectrum may be added in a future version.)

5. Mode Mixing and Ensemble EMD (EEMD)

The most extensively discussed practical limitation of EMD is the phenomenon of mode mixing: distinct frequency components may appear within a single IMF over different time intervals, or the same frequency component may be distributed across several IMFs. This behaviour is observed especially for intermittent signals in which the distribution of local extrema is irregular. Ensemble EMD (EEMD) addresses this issue by adding a large number of independent realisations of white noise to the signal, applying EMD to each, and averaging the resulting IMFs (Wu & Huang, 2009). The CEEMDAN algorithm provides a more refined noise-addition strategy.

📍 6. Application Areas

  • Seismology & Structural Engineering: Decomposition of earthquake records, building/bridge health monitoring, extraction of natural vibration modes (Huang & Wu, 2008).
  • Biomedical Signals: Decomposition of sleep stages in EEG, separation of QRS complex from baseline in ECG, EMG signal cleaning, fMRI BOLD signal analysis.
  • Mechanical Fault Diagnosis: Early detection of periodic faults in bearings and gearboxes — wind turbines, jet engines, trailer axles.
  • Financial Time Series: Separating market trend from cyclical components, stock volatility analysis, trend detection for currency pairs.
  • Oceanography & Climate: Ocean wave measurements, El Nino/ENSO signal, trend & seasonal component separation in climate series.
  • Speech & Audio Processing: Formant extraction, noise suppression, separation of transient and sustained components in music.
  • Image Processing: Texture-trend separation with 2D EMD, image enhancement, segmentation.
  • Astronomy: Sunspot cycles, gamma-ray bursts, pulsar light curve analysis.

7. Implementation on This Page

This page hosts an EMD implementation written in pure JavaScript. Three signal sources are offered: (a) multi-component synthetic signals (for example, the sum of 5 Hz and 30 Hz sinusoids — the classical EMD demonstration); (b) signals drawn by the user with the mouse; (c) audio recordings uploaded or captured via the microphone (up to 3 s, subject to anti-aliasing filtered down-sampling). The sifting procedure is carried out by means of cubic-spline envelopes; each IMF is extracted with at most 12 iterations or when $\mathrm{SD} < 0.2$ is satisfied.

Stacked-layer view: as the corresponding slider is moved, the original signal is unfolded from top to bottom, followed in order by IMF$_1$, IMF$_2$, … and the residual at the bottom. The Reconstruction slider visualises the cumulative sum $\hat{x}_K = r + \sum_{i=1}^{K} \mathrm{IMF}_i$; when $K = M$ is reached, the identity $\hat{x} = x$ holds and the resulting RMSE remains at machine-precision level. Both the original and the reconstructed signals may be played back, and the reconstruction may be downloaded in .wav format.
