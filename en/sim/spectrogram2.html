Windowing, Overlap, and STFT — An Engineering Perspective

The classical DFT, defined by $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\, e^{-j 2\pi kn/N}$, presupposes a signal of infinite length and stationary behaviour. Real-world signals (speech, music, vibration, radar returns), in contrast, exhibit time-varying characteristics. This limitation is addressed by partitioning the signal into short windows and computing the DFT of each window separately; the resulting procedure is referred to as the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT): $$\boxed{\, X[m, k] \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[mH + n]\, w[n]\, e^{-j 2\pi kn/N} \,}.$$

  • $N$: the FFT length; the frequency resolution is given by $\Delta f = f_s/N$.
  • $H$: the hop size between consecutive windows; $H = N\,(1-\text{overlap ratio})$ samples.
  • $w[n]$: the window function, applied in order to smooth the discontinuities at the frame boundaries.

1. The Need for Windowing

Truncating a signal with a rectangular window corresponds, in the frequency domain, to convolution with the $\mathrm{sinc}$ function. Since the side lobes of $\mathrm{sinc}$ lie only −13 dB below the main lobe, pronounced spectral leakage appears around the true tone. Window functions are used to suppress this leakage; in return, a broadening of the main lobe (and hence a degradation of the frequency resolution) is observed.

3. The Role of the Overlap Ratio

The window function decays towards zero at its edges, so that the signal information in those regions is effectively suppressed. Overlap ensures that consecutive windows partially cover one another; consequently, each sample is represented with weight in several windows. When the Constant OverLap Add (COLA) condition is satisfied, the signal can be perfectly reconstructed.

  • 0% overlap ($H=N$): the procedure is fast but information is lost at the window boundaries, and COLA is not satisfied.
  • 50% overlap ($H=N/2$): the COLA condition is satisfied for the Hann window and this configuration is sufficient for the majority of applications.
  • 75% overlap ($H=N/4$): produces a smoother stream and is well suited to Blackman-type windows.
  • 87.5% overlap ($H=N/8$): yields high temporal resolution and is preferred in machine-learning and audio-synthesis applications.

4. Heisenberg-Type Uncertainty

The inequality $\Delta t \cdot \Delta f \geq 1/(4\pi)$ implies that the time and frequency resolutions cannot be jointly improved. Increasing the FFT length $N$ improves the frequency resolution ($\Delta f = f_s/N$) but degrades the temporal resolution ($\Delta t = N/f_s$). The overlap ratio enhances visual smoothness along the time axis without genuinely improving the temporal resolution; it merely increases the sampling density.

Application Notes

  • Speech analysis: a Hamming window with 50% overlap constitutes a standard configuration in MFCC computation.
  • Music analysis: a Hann window with 75% overlap yields a smooth spectrogram stream.
  • Measurement applications: the Flat-top window is preferred for amplitude calibration, since it permits the true amplitude of sinusoidal peaks to be read directly.
  • Frequency separation: two closely spaced tones (e.g. 1000 Hz and 1020 Hz) merge under the Hann window but can be resolved with Blackman-Harris (the main-lobe / side-lobe trade-off).
  • Low SNR: when side-lobe suppression is critical, Nuttall or Blackman-Harris windows are recommended.

A Suggested Experiment

With the "Close Tones (1000 + 1020 Hz)" preset selected, the Rectangular window produces a single broad peak in which the two tones cannot be resolved. Switching to Hann reveals two distinct peaks together with visible side lobes; the Blackman-Harris window yields a clean separation of the two peaks. Finally, with the Flat-top window the main lobe broadens while the peak amplitudes can be read with calibrated accuracy.

DTMF (Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency) Key Codes

When you press a key on a telephone, two pure sinusoids are produced simultaneously: one is a "row" frequency (697–941 Hz), the other is a "column" frequency (1209–1633 Hz). The two tones are specifically chosen to have no harmonic relationship — they cannot be confused with vocal cords, music, or noise. Window Experiments with the Custom Signal Builder

  • Dynamic range: Add 1000 Hz @ 0 dB + 1100 Hz @ -60 dB. With Rectangular, the weak tone (1100) is buried in the side lobes of the strong one. Switch to Blackman-Harris → it emerges.
  • 3-tone resolution: 1000, 1015, 1040 Hz all at 0 dB. Only Blackman-Harris or Nuttall shows three separate peaks. Others produce a merged hump.
  • Off-bin scalloping: Single component with frequency not at a bin center (e.g. 1023.456 Hz). With Hann the peak reads slightly low; switch to Flat-top and the peak amplitude "snaps" to the correct value.
  • Phase cancellation: Add 440 Hz @ 0 dB, φ=0° and 440 Hz @ 0 dB, φ=180°. The spectrum should be nearly empty (signal power ≈ 0). Set φ to 90° and power returns.
  • Beating: 440 + 442 Hz two components → audible 2 Hz amplitude modulation; two close peaks in the spectrum. Increase FFT to 8192 to see the separation.
