Signal Processing Framework of Doppler Radar

A radar system transmits a pulse train or a continuous wave (CW) at a known carrier frequency $f_c$, and the echo scattered from a moving target returns with a Doppler shift $f_d$ that is directly proportional to the target's radial velocity. The signal-processing chain at the receiver measures this small frequency shift and determines the velocity of the target along the radar line of sight.

1) Doppler Shift — Fundamental Relation

With the radial velocity $v_r$ taken as positive towards the radar, the two-way path doubles the shift and introduces a factor of 2$\times$ in the fundamental relation:

$$ f_d \;=\; \frac{2\,v_r}{\lambda} \;=\; \frac{2\,v_r\,f_c}{c}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f_c} $$
  • For an approaching target (towards the radar) $f_d > 0$, whereas for a receding target $f_d < 0$.
  • If $\theta$ denotes the angle between the radar line of sight and the target's velocity vector, the radial velocity is given by $v_r = |\vec{v}|\cos\theta$.
  • At $\theta = 90°$ (tangential flight) the radial velocity vanishes ($v_r = 0$), producing a Doppler blind spot in which the target peak falls onto the DC bin of the spectrum.

2) Signal Model

The transmitted carrier and the received echo are modelled in idealised form as follows:

$$ s_{TX}(t) = \cos(2\pi f_c\,t), \qquad s_{RX}(t) = A\,\cos\!\big(2\pi(f_c + f_d)(t - \tau)\big) + n(t) + c(t) $$
  • $\tau = 2R/c$ — the round-trip delay, which carries the range information.
  • $n(t)$ — thermal noise (AWGN); its power is controlled by the SNR parameter.
  • $c(t)$ — clutter: strong echoes returning from stationary scatterers such as the ground, sea surface, rain or buildings; they accumulate around $f \approx 0$ in the spectrum.

3) Processing Chain

STEP 1
RF Mixing (Mixer)
$s_{RX}(t)$ is multiplied with the carrier at the receiver → intermediate frequency (IF) or I/Q baseband. The Doppler difference $f_d$ is now at baseband.
STEP 2
Range Gate
A sample window corresponding to delay $\tau$ is selected. Only target contributions at that range pass through.
STEP 3
Pulse-to-Pulse Sampling
At the end of each PRI (= 1/PRF), one complex sample is collected. Doppler processing operates on this slow-time sequence.
STEP 4
MTI / High-Pass
Difference filter to suppress stationary clutter: $h[n]=\{1,-1\}$ type, $y[n]=x[n]-x[n-1]$. DC is nulled.
STEP 5
Window + FFT
Hann/Hamming window → $N$-point FFT. Bin resolution $\Delta f = \text{PRF}/N$.
STEP 6
Peak Detection (CFAR)
Strongest bin in spectrum → $\hat{f}_d$. Velocity: $\hat v_r = c\,\hat f_d / (2 f_c)$.

4) Ambiguity and Resolution Limits

  • Doppler ambiguity: a pulsed radar samples the slow-time axis at the PRF; consequently, by the Nyquist criterion, the shift can only be resolved unambiguously within $|f_d| < \text{PRF}/2$. Doppler values outside this interval are folded back (aliasing) and lead to erroneous velocity readings.
  • The corresponding ambiguous velocity is $v_{\max} = \dfrac{c\,\text{PRF}}{4 f_c}$ and is reported live in the upper panel.
  • Velocity resolution: according to $\Delta v = \dfrac{c}{2 f_c}\cdot\dfrac{\text{PRF}}{N}$, the resolution improves as the observation length $N$ increases.
  • SNR and detection: in order for the target peak to be distinguishable from the noise floor, a sufficient integration gain must be achieved. Coherent integration over $N$ samples increases the SNR by $10\log_{10} N$ dB.

5) Quantities Visualised in This Simulation

  1. Scene panel: a top-down view is provided; the speed $|\vec v|$ and heading angle $\phi$ of the aircraft are controlled by the user. The radar illuminates the target with concentric wave rings and the echo is returned.
  2. $s_{TX}, s_{RX}$ panel: the transmitted carrier and the received echo are shown in the time domain. Since the Doppler shift is small, it is not directly visible to the naked eye; however, the phase accumulated within each PRI is extracted by the Doppler-processing chain.
  3. Doppler spectrum: the FFT of the slow-time sequence is computed; the target peak appears at $f_d$, while clutter (when present) appears as a broad peak around $f = 0$ on a uniformly distributed noise floor.
  4. Waterfall: the evolution of the spectrum over time is displayed. As the heading of the target is changed, the peak line may be observed to shift and to cross the "zero-Doppler" condition at $f = 0$.

6) Suggested Experiments

  • The SNR may be set to −15 dB to observe whether the target peak becomes buried in noise; the corresponding case with SNR = ∞ may then be used for comparison.
  • Under high-clutter conditions, the MTI filter may be activated and deactivated in turn so as to study the suppression of the DC peak.
  • When the aircraft is steered to pass directly beside the radar, the sign reversal of $v_r$ can be tracked on the waterfall display.
  • The carrier frequency $f_c$ may be varied from 0.5 GHz up to 35 GHz so as to observe how $f_d$ scales for a fixed velocity (the wavelength $\lambda$ decreases accordingly).
  • By lowering the PRF, the ambiguous velocity may be brought below the actual velocity of the aircraft, so that the folding of the spectral peak can be directly observed.
