Laplace Transform — $s$-Plane & Relationship with Fourier

Fundamental Relationship

The Laplace transform is defined over the complex variable $s = \sigma + j\Omega$ through the relation $X(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,e^{-st}\,dt$ and represents the signal in terms of generalised complex exponentials. The Fourier transform $X(j\Omega) = \int x(t)\,e^{-j\Omega t}\,dt$, in contrast, is defined only on the $\sigma = 0$ axis — that is, on the imaginary axis: $$X(j\Omega) = X(s)\big|_{s=j\Omega}.$$

Geometric interpretation: the cross-section of the three-dimensional $|H(s)|$ surface taken along the $j\Omega$ axis corresponds to the frequency response $|H(j\Omega)|$. On this page, that cross-section is plotted in real time on the lower graph; the moving red marker traverses the $j\Omega$ axis and simultaneously visualises the vectors directed towards each pole and zero.

Algebraic expression: $$|H(j\Omega)| = \frac{\prod_i |j\Omega - z_i|}{\prod_j |j\Omega - p_j|},$$ so that the magnitude of the frequency response is interpreted as the ratio of the product of distances from $j\Omega$ to the zeros over the product of distances to the poles.

Poles and Zeros

  • Pole ($\times$): the points at which $H(s)$ diverges to infinity; they appear as peaks on the $s$-plane surface.
  • Zero ($\circ$): the points at which $H(s) = 0$; they appear as valleys on the surface.
  • Stability condition: all poles must satisfy $\mathrm{Re}\{s\} = \sigma < 0$, that is, they must lie in the left half-plane.
  • Complex conjugacy: in systems with real coefficients, complex poles and zeros invariably occur in conjugate pairs; consequently, for any pole or zero defined at $\Omega \neq 0$, the corresponding conjugate is added to the system automatically.

Application Areas

  • Control engineering: system design via transfer functions and closed-loop stability analysis are carried out in motor, robot and aircraft control.
  • Circuit analysis: the design of RLC, op-amp and active filter circuits is performed using network equations involving capacitors and inductors.
  • Mechanical systems: mass–spring–damper models, vibration isolation design and seismic protection are addressed within this framework.
  • Signal processing: analogue filter design (Butterworth, Chebyshev, elliptic) and continuous-time system realisations are conducted in the Laplace domain.
  • Audio and acoustics: loudspeaker equalisation and room-acoustic correction filters are designed through pole–zero placement.
  • Biomedical: ECG pre-filtering, cardiac rhythm models and drug-distribution kinetics (pharmacokinetics) are analysed using the Laplace transform.
  • Power systems: generator stability and feedback design are performed within this framework.
