Sampling Theorem — Time vs Frequency

The sampling of a continuous-time signal $x(t)$ is modelled mathematically as multiplication by a Dirac comb: $$x_s(t) \;=\; x(t)\cdot p(t),\qquad p(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-kT_s).$$ Multiplication in the time domain corresponds to convolution in the frequency domain. Furthermore, the Fourier transform of the impulse train is again an impulse train, with spacing $F_s = 1/T_s$: $$P(F)=F_s\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(F-kF_s).$$ Consequently, the spectrum of the sampled signal is obtained as $$X_s(F)=X(F)*P(F)=F_s\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X(F-kF_s).$$ In other words, multiplication by a Dirac comb in the time domain corresponds to convolution with the same comb in the frequency domain, and the spectrum is therefore replicated periodically with period $F_s$.

Nyquist–Shannon condition: if $x(t)$ is band-limited (i.e. $X(F) = 0$ for $|F| > B$), the spectrum copies remain disjoint provided that $$F_s \;>\; 2B \quad(\text{Nyquist rate})$$ is satisfied. If this condition is violated, adjacent copies overlap at baseband, giving rise to aliasing. In this simulation, the region in which the overlap occurs is highlighted in the bottom-right spectrum graph.

Use of this page: a preset may be selected from the left panel (alternatively, $x(t)$ may be drawn directly on the graph via the ✏ Freehand option); the parameters $\Delta t$ (fine grid resolution) and $N$ are adjustable. In the central panel, either the sampling period $T_s$ or the sampling frequency $F_s$ is set. When the condition $F_s < 2 f_0$ holds, the spectrum copies begin to overlap and the red aliasing indicator is activated.

Application Areas

  • Digital audio: the CD standard rate of 44.1 kHz and professional rates of 96/192 kHz are chosen in relation to the approximate 20 kHz upper limit of human hearing and the anti-aliasing filter.
  • Imaging and camera systems: the pixel grid constitutes a sampling lattice; low-frequency moiré patterns are the visual manifestation of aliasing.
  • Software-defined radio (SDR): anti-aliasing low-pass filtering is performed prior to the ADC, and bandpass sampling techniques are employed.
  • Medical signal processing: in ECG, EEG and ultrasound measurements, the sampling rate is selected in accordance with the Nyquist condition.
  • Industrial measurement: in vibration sensors and rotating-machinery monitoring, aliasing may give rise to erroneous fault identification.
