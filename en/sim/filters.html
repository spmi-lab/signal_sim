Filters — frequency-selective LTI systems

A filter is an LTI system whose frequency response $H$ passes some frequency bands and attenuates others. An analog filter is described by the transfer function $H(s)=B(s)/A(s)$ in the Laplace domain; a digital filter by $H(z)=B(z)/A(z)$ in the $z$-domain. The design here starts from a normalised analog low-pass prototype, applies a low-pass→{LP, HP, BP, BS} frequency transform, and — in digital mode — maps the result to the $z$-plane with the pre-warped bilinear transform $s=\tfrac{2}{T}\tfrac{z-1}{z+1}$.

Approximation families:
Butterworth — maximally flat passband, monotonic; gentle roll-off.
Chebyshev I — equiripple in the passband, steeper roll-off (set the ripple $R_p$).
Chebyshev II — flat passband, equiripple stopband with transmission zeros (set the attenuation $R_s$).
FIR (windowed sinc) — exactly linear phase (constant group delay), unconditionally stable, only zeros.

What to look for: Magnitude — the $-3\,\mathrm{dB}$ line meets the curve at the cutoff; raising the order steepens the transition band. Pole–Zero — an IIR digital filter is stable when all poles lie inside the unit circle; an analog filter when they lie in the left half-plane. Notches sit where zeros touch the unit circle / the $j\Omega$ axis. Phase / group delay — IIR filters bend the phase, FIR filters keep it linear (a flat group delay = no waveform distortion). Live demo — the bold output keeps the passband tone while the stopband tone and broadband noise collapse into the noise floor.

Where filters are used

  • Anti-aliasing & reconstruction: band-limiting before sampling and smoothing after reconstruction (see the Sampling module).
  • Audio & communications: equalisers, channel selection, the low-pass demodulation filter in the Radio module.
  • Biomedical & instrumentation: 50/60 Hz notch (band-stop) removal, baseline-wander high-pass.
  • Radar & sensing: the MTI clutter filter in the Doppler module is a high-pass filter on slow-time.
