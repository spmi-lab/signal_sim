CTFT — Continuous-Time Fourier Transform

For an (aperiodic, absolutely integrable) signal $x(t)$, the Continuous-Time Fourier Transform and its inverse are $$X(j\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,e^{-j\Omega t}\,dt,\qquad x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X(j\Omega)\,e^{j\Omega t}\,d\Omega.$$ The result is a continuous, generally complex function of $\Omega$, displayed here through its magnitude $|X(j\Omega)|$ and phase $\angle X(j\Omega)$. The transform is computed numerically (a Riemann sum of the analysis integral), and for the library signals the exact closed form is overlaid as a dashed reference.

Common pairs:
$\mathrm{rect}_W(t)\;\leftrightarrow\;W\,\mathrm{sinc}(\Omega W/2)$
$e^{-t^2/2\sigma^2}\;\leftrightarrow\;\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{-\sigma^2\Omega^2/2}$ (Gaussian → Gaussian)
$e^{-at}u(t)\;\leftrightarrow\;\dfrac{1}{a+j\Omega}$
$e^{-a|t|}\;\leftrightarrow\;\dfrac{2a}{a^2+\Omega^2}$

Properties to observe: Duality — switch between the rect and sinc signals: a narrow pulse in time gives a wide spectrum and vice-versa. Time shift — moving $t_0$ leaves $|X|$ unchanged but adds the linear phase $-\Omega t_0$. Modulation — the windowed cosine and the Gabor signal shift the baseband spectrum to $\pm\Omega_0$. Scaling — widening the pulse compresses the spectrum (the time–bandwidth product is conserved).

Relationship with the other transforms

  • Fourier series: as the period $T\to\infty$, the discrete spectral lines of a periodic signal merge into the continuous CTFT.
  • Laplace transform: the CTFT is the Laplace transform evaluated on the imaginary axis, $X(j\Omega)=X(s)\big|_{s=j\Omega}$, when the region of convergence contains that axis.
  • DTFT: sampling $x(t)$ produces the $2\pi$-periodic DTFT, the periodic replication of this spectrum at the sampling rate.
  • Filtering: the frequency response $H(j\Omega)$ of an analog filter is exactly the CTFT of its impulse response $h(t)$.
