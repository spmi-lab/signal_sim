DFT / FFT — Digital Spectrum Analysis

The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) of a discrete-time sequence of $N$ samples is defined by $$X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N},\quad k=0,1,\dots,N-1$$ together with its inverse (IDFT) $$x[n]=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]\,e^{+j2\pi kn/N}.$$ The DFT corresponds to the DTFT sampled at the points $\omega_k = 2\pi k/N$, so that $X[k] = X(e^{j\omega_k})$.

Zero-padding: when the sequence $x[n]$ is extended with zeros so that $N_{\text{fft}} > N$, the DFT samples the DTFT more densely. The procedure introduces no additional frequency information; it merely increases the apparent resolution of the displayed spectrum.

Spectral leakage: when $x[n]$ is not exactly periodic over the length $N$, the DFT fails to recover the ideal $\delta$ components of the spectrum of the underlying continuous sinusoid; the signal energy spreads to neighbouring frequency bins. Window functions such as Hann or Hamming smooth the discontinuities at the frame boundaries, thereby reducing the side-lobe level at the cost of a broadened main lobe.

The FFT (Cooley–Tukey algorithm) computes the DFT with complexity $\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$, in contrast with the $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ complexity of the direct formula. 📍 Application Areas

  • Audio and music: equalisation, pitch shifting and vocoder operations are performed through the short-time FFT (STFT).
  • Image processing: the two-dimensional FFT serves as the fundamental tool for filtering, compression and pattern analysis.
  • Telecommunications: the modulation of OFDM symbols (Wi-Fi, 5G) is carried out directly through IFFT/FFT operations.
  • Science and engineering: pulsar period estimation in astronomy, k-space analysis in MRI, and vibration/acoustic analyses are based on this transform.
  • Machine learning: spectrogram and mel-spectrogram representations as well as FFT-based fast-convolution implementations make extensive use of the DFT/FFT.
