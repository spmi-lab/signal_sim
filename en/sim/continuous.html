Continuous-Time Convolution

$$y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$$ The expression above defines the convolution integral between the input signal $x(t)$ and the impulse response $h(t)$. In the third panel, the $h$ signal may be dragged with the mouse, so that the resulting product integral for each value of $t$ can be observed directly. The red marker indicates the location on the $y(t)$ curve in the fourth panel corresponding to the current integral value.

Notation (continuous time): the independent variable is taken as $t \in \mathbb{R}$, the angular frequency as $\Omega \in \mathbb{R}$ (rad/s), and the ordinary frequency as $F = \Omega/(2\pi)$ (Hz).

📍 Application Areas

  • Circuit analysis: The transient response of RC, RL and RLC circuits is obtained as the convolution of the input voltage with the impulse response.
  • Mechanics: The response of mass–spring–damper systems under impulsive excitation (earthquake–structural dynamics) is modelled within this framework.
  • Control engineering: The time-domain response of a continuous-time LTI system to a given reference input is computed by means of this convolution.
  • Optics and imaging: The image recorded by the camera is obtained as the convolution of the scene with the point spread function (PSF).
  • Acoustics: The convolution of the source signal with the room impulse response (RIR) constitutes the basis of reverberation simulation.
  • Radar and sonar: The received echo is expressed as the convolution of the transmitted pulse with the impulse response of the target.
