Z-Transform — $z$-Plane & DTFT on the Unit Circle

Fundamental Relationship

The Z-transform of a discrete-time signal $x[n]$ is defined by $$X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\, z^{-n},$$ where the complex variable $z = r\,e^{j\omega}$ denotes a point in the $z$-plane. The Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT) corresponds to the evaluation of $z$ exclusively on the unit circle ($|z| = 1$, $z = e^{j\omega}$): $$X(e^{j\omega}) = X(z)\big|_{z = e^{j\omega}} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\, e^{-j\omega n}.$$

Geometric interpretation: the cross-section of the three-dimensional $|H(z)|$ surface taken along the unit circle directly yields the frequency response $|H(e^{j\omega})|$. This cross-section is plotted in real time on the second graph below; the angular variable $\omega$ may be swept around the unit circle by means of the play control in the left panel.

Algebraic expression: $$|H(e^{j\omega})| = \frac{\prod_i |e^{j\omega} - z_i|}{\prod_j |e^{j\omega} - p_j|},$$ so that the magnitude of the DTFT is interpreted as the ratio of the product of distances from $z = e^{j\omega}$ to the zeros over the product of distances to the poles.

Poles and Zeros

  • Pole ($\times$): the points at which $H(z)$ diverges; they form peaks on the surface and pronounced peaks in the frequency response.
  • Zero ($\circ$): the points satisfying $H(z) = 0$; they form valleys on the surface and notches in the frequency response.
  • Stability condition (causal LTI systems): all poles must lie inside the unit circle, i.e. $|p_j| < 1$. A pole lying outside the unit circle leads $h[n]$ to grow without bound as $n \to \infty$.
  • Complex conjugacy: in systems with real coefficients, complex poles and zeros always occur in conjugate pairs; consequently, for any pole or zero with $\mathrm{Im}(z) \neq 0$, the corresponding conjugate is added to the system automatically.

Comparison with the Laplace Transform

Continuous-time systems are analysed in the $s$-plane, where stability is characterised by the left half-plane ($\mathrm{Re}\{s\} < 0$). In discrete-time systems, the $z$-plane is employed and stability is confined to the interior of the unit circle ($|z| < 1$). The continuous-time $j\Omega$ axis corresponds to the unit circle in discrete time, with the mapping $$s = j\Omega \;\;\longleftrightarrow\;\; z = e^{j\omega}, \quad \omega = \Omega T_s,$$ in which $T_s$ denotes the sampling period.

Difference Equation and $h[n]$

From the expression $$H(z) = \frac{B(z)}{A(z)} = \frac{\sum_k b_k\, z^{-k}}{1 + \sum_{k \geq 1} a_k\, z^{-k}}$$ the difference equation is obtained directly: $$y[n] = \sum_k b_k\, x[n-k] - \sum_{k \geq 1} a_k\, y[n-k].$$ By taking $x[n] = \delta[n]$ as the excitation and applying the recursion, the impulse response $h[n]$ is obtained and is displayed in the stem plot at the bottom right. In the signal module below, the discrete convolution $y[n] = h[n] * x[n]$ is computed directly via the FFT.

📍 Application Areas

  • Digital filter design: FIR and IIR filters, as well as the discrete-time counterparts of the Butterworth, Chebyshev and elliptic designs obtained through the bilinear transform, are constructed within this framework.
  • Speech and audio processing: equalisation, Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) based audio compression and noise suppression are realised through Z-domain tools.
  • Image processing: two-dimensional discrete filters used for edge detection, blurring and sharpening are designed via this model.
  • Digital control: discrete-time PID controllers, state-space controllers and autopilot algorithms are analysed in the $z$-plane.
  • Telecommunications and modems: carrier tracking, digital demodulators, decision-feedback equalisers (DFE) and OFDM channel estimation are conducted within this framework.
  • Biomedical signal processing: digital filtering of ECG/EEG, heartbeat detection and noise removal are performed using Z-transform-based systems.
  • Econometrics and finance: ARMA-based time-series forecasting and digital Kalman filtering are addressed within this approach.
