DTFT — Discrete-Time Fourier Transform

For a discrete-time signal $x[n]$, the Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT) is defined by $$X(e^{j\omega})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,e^{-j\omega n}.$$ The result is a continuous and $2\pi$-periodic function of $\omega$, satisfying $X(e^{j(\omega+2\pi)})=X(e^{j\omega})$. This periodicity follows from the identity $e^{-j(\omega+2\pi)n}=e^{-j\omega n}\,e^{-j2\pi n}$, since $e^{-j2\pi n}=1$ for every integer $n$.

Fundamental DTFT pairs:
$\delta[n]\;\leftrightarrow\;1$
$a^n\,u[n], \; |a|<1\;\leftrightarrow\;\dfrac{1}{1-a\,e^{-j\omega}}$
$\text{rect}_M[n] \;\leftrightarrow\;\dfrac{\sin(\omega(2M+1)/2)}{\sin(\omega/2)}$ (Dirichlet kernel)
$\cos(\omega_0 n)\;\leftrightarrow\;\pi\!\sum_k\!\big[\delta(\omega-\omega_0+2\pi k)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0+2\pi k)\big]$

Geometric interpretation: upon the substitution $z = e^{j\omega}$, the DTFT corresponds to the evaluation of the Z-transform on the unit circle. As the point on the circle in the right panel is dragged, $\omega$ varies and the red cursor in the lower graphs is updated synchronously; a full revolution of $2\pi$ on the circle corresponds to one complete period on the frequency axis.

Reading the values: at the cursor position $\omega$ the magnitude $|X(e^{j\omega})|$ and the phase $\angle X(e^{j\omega})$ are displayed. When the periodicity highlight is enabled, the spectrum copies at $\pm 2\pi$ are shown in a distinct shade; this visualisation emphasises the property that most clearly distinguishes the DTFT from the continuous-time Fourier transform.

Application Areas

  • Filter design: the frequency response $H(e^{j\omega})$ of FIR and IIR filters is defined through the DTFT.
  • Digital signal analysis: the "ideal" spectral estimate (with an infinite window) of discrete-time samples is examined within this framework.
  • Relationship with the DFT/FFT: the DFT, as computed in practice, corresponds to the DTFT sampled at the points $\omega = 2\pi k/N$.
  • Relationship with the Z-transform: provided that the region of convergence (ROC) of the Z-transform includes the unit circle, the DTFT converges.
